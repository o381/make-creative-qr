# Composition, appearance review and failure diagnosis

## Before creation: give distribution an origin

Use three sentences to define an independent event or abstract rule, the formation process carrying information, and at least two compositional differences from the previous work. A theme name is not a composition. A new character or object surrounding the same central grid and three special clusters repeats the layout.

Natural integration comes from material and spatial relationships, not photographic realism or random noise. Exaggeration, fantasy and two-dimensional scenes are welcome, but gathering, dispersal, contact, motion and layering must follow the image's own rules. Do not retrofit a story to an existing code block.

For a brief seeking incidental scene integration, distinguish an event from an arrangement illustrating that event. A connecting strip or a poetic title does not explain separately shaped props, regular spacing or a silhouette that required deliberate cutting. Before rendering texture, ask what visible action or physical process produces the distribution. Judge this separately from correct shadows and realistic materials. A staged craft direction may suit other briefs; do not treat it as spontaneous integration by default.

Before building the guide, check three scales:

| Scale | What to design | False naturalness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole image | Origin, destination, hierarchy, density, boundaries and relationship to the event | A central rectangle of encoding, a character beside it and decorations around it |
| Clusters | Size variation, gathering, dispersal, direction and contact | Three matching corner clusters, or arbitrary matching clusters added as camouflage |
| Details | Particle sizes, edges, gaps, gloss and material continuity | Fraying, particles and noise on the same unchanged code-shaped composition |

Do not arbitrarily randomize exact information positions. Explore valid grouping, new matrices for the same content, whole-scene layout and plausible continuation in safe exterior areas. Verify every change.

Match the carrier's natural detail scale and dominant directions to the required information before generation. Compare functional-region sizes with ordinary material features separately from data-detail sizes; credible texture cannot hide three disproportionately large motifs. Consider a valid new encoding or layout when their scales conflict, then recheck the complete actual file rather than assuming a denser matrix remains readable. Broad radial cloth folds, for example, do not automatically support dense changes in both directions. Before fitting a matrix or minimizing pixel changes, identify where the observed material actually exists: empty mist, paper or background can be numerically cheap to alter but turn the encoding into newly added dots. Reject such placements before optimizing. A model may make the material more credible by removing unsupported changes. When that happens, compare the actual geometry with the guide; do not force the missing information back as a flat tonal patch. Choose a formation process that can plausibly produce the needed fine structure, then validate its actual rendering.

## Compare works: extract differences without copying layouts

These are transferable comparison methods, not proven recipes:

- Density transitions, varied cluster sizes and related particles crossing the information boundary may reduce the dedicated-panel impression, provided they share one formation process and preserve necessary tonal gaps.
- Smooth, heavy continuous contours, nearly uniform line widths and regular holes can expose a designed pattern. Continuous material is not inherently a failure; check whether it reveals a rectangular subject and module rhythm.
- Ragged edges or powder may soften local contours while leaving the same dense square. More complex texture does not establish a more natural composition.
- Thin lines, tails or branches connecting locator regions change connectivity only. Reconstruct if the three positions, reserved gaps and information boundary still stand out.
- Adding similar clusters elsewhere is not an automatic solution. Camouflage without material origin, density transitions or scene relationships can look even more contrived.
- Natural material and a clear event are separate judgments. After a material change, recheck whether action, contact and behavior explain each other. Do not retain a rougher surface at the expense of a coherent event.

Preserve the user's comparison hierarchy. “The first is more natural than the second, but still forced” expresses both preference and reservation, not full integration. Extract useful mechanisms and record unresolved structural defects.

## Review the whole image before details

Inspect the complete image and an approximately 320-pixel-wide thumbnail before zooming into materials. Thumbnails assess appearance, never scanning. Check in order:

Judge the thumbnail without its title or creation narrative. If rich material detail distracts from the layout, also inspect a simplified three-tone thumbnail for density boundaries and three unusually prominent features. This auxiliary view diagnoses composition only; it cannot approve decoding or replace review of the actual artwork. A polished surface must not carry a previously rejected distribution through this check.

1. **Independent composition:** without mentioning scanning, can a viewer understand the event, material origin and distribution? Does an abstract image have its own rule?
2. **No dedicated code surface:** does the whole still look like a dense rectangle, even with extended lines or ragged edges? Is the exterior merely decorative? Do not crop away the scene to evade this check.
3. **No special treatment for three corners:** compare upper-left, upper-right and lower-left regions with the whole for size, sharpness, grayscale, gaps and surrounding particles. Unique clusters, satellite dots, blurred shadows, icons, rings and cleared areas require revision, even if they are not square.
4. **Continuity across the boundary:** are materials, particle-size ranges, thickness and gloss consistent, with plausible density transitions? Tiny flat central marks surrounded only by large three-dimensional pieces still expose the code area.
5. **Intact elements:** have patterns, characters, geometry or figures become meaningless fused shapes? If recognizable form and encoding cannot coexist, change the carrier mechanism.
6. **Real variation between works:** ignoring color and background, have formation, gathering, direction, boundaries and scene relationships changed? A different mask or character is insufficient.
7. **Material and optical credibility:** inspect contact, occlusion, light direction and shadows separately from decoding. Solid pieces need plausible support and contact shadows where the lighting calls for them; projected or refracted light should follow the receiving surface rather than acquire the relief of a floating coating. Check native-size texture and edges for smearing, halos and lost detail; a convincing thumbnail does not establish sharp material rendering. Fine structure should follow its material: pigment boundaries must not independently redirect fur, fibers or surface grain. A patch of radial tufts or changed texture orientation can remain artificial despite sharp pixels.

When protecting or moving anatomy, use the observed element silhouette. A broad source selection can carry background pixels into another surface, create false holes and inflate the apparent protected error budget. Inspect the composited material before treating that selection as an encoding constraint. When known geometry permits a diagnostic error budget, count damaged codewords in each correction block and functional-region damage separately; a small total area or average module agreement can hide an overloaded block. Use this check before another repaint or encoding fit. Known-geometry sampling guides revisions but never qualifies an artwork.

For every failure, record its specific location and relationship. Boundary, event or distribution failures need composition changes; local material issues may use small edits. Do not repeatedly reskin the same subject layout. Apply this review before asking for a phone retest too: when the brief is finished natural artwork, a known visible grid or three processed targets sends the image back to creation, even if software robustness improved. A diagnostic label does not change that decision; user-requested investigation previews remain possible.

After tonal corrections, check whether the surface appears to have changed shape: a smooth brightness field can turn sharp facets into soft dents or produce three padded targets while leaving the original pixels sharp. When transferring fine texture across changed pigmentation, distinguish material grain from old pigment edges: a high-frequency residual can carry the previous pattern back as ghosts. Preserve the observed surface boundaries and material behaviour in the correction or redraw. Pixel sharpness and a small pixel-change cost do not establish an unedited-looking material; inspect the altered structures in context, and repeat both appearance review and complete decoding.

For color-based information, compare the complete composition before and after palette or luminance normalization. Flattened hierarchy, washed colors and a faint bounded code panel are visual failures even when decoding improves. Lower contrast alone does not integrate the information; inspect the thumbnail before extending contrast or density experiments.

## Feedback and example qualification

Keep the following states separate in private task records, bound to the actual file and hash or host-guaranteed immutable asset version:

| State | Valid evidence | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relative preference | Which image the user prefers, ranking and remaining defects | Visual acceptance |
| Appearance judgment | Whole-image review and specific user feedback | Readability or an actual phone test |
| Software qualification | Real decoding of the current complete file and all required variants | Guaranteed phone app scanning |
| Phone evidence | Actual user or operator feedback, corresponding file and method | Acceptance of another version, next artwork or other scanning methods |

A liked or collected image without complete size testing can remain a visual reference, not a qualified scannable showcase. Reverify the actual uploaded copy. A user's failed phone scan has veto power; software success cannot override it.

If a phone-rejected version also has reproducible failures in an available independent decoder or modest whole-image capture diagnostics, carry the relevant failures into the revision's regression checks. Passing the old size profile alone is insufficient reason to request another phone test while those same failures persist. Keep the known-good diagnostic repair as a causal control, not as permission to deliver conspicuous locator symbols.

After a reported phone failure, retain the failed asset and scanning method as a regression case. Compare functional-region-only and data-region-only repairs privately to isolate detection from content recovery; obvious diagnostic symbols are not deliverables. When simplifying locators, inspect the complete contour and exterior light separation as well as the central cross-sections; a readable cross alone does not establish robust detection. A single engine passing many sizes can still share one detection weakness. Test the revision with an independent implementation and modest whole-image rotation or capture-like degradation where available, record disagreements, and keep the actual phone result unresolved until retested. These diagnostics supplement the original acceptance profile; they neither replace the phone test nor justify restoring three conspicuous corner targets.

## Choose revisions from evidence

| Observation or evidence | Next action |
| --- | --- |
| Guide passes; actual artwork fails every test | Compare whole-scene scale, offset, module centers and functional grayscale. Repair information drift before adding background detail. |
| Repaint reproduces a material reference's old arrangement instead of the information guide | Remove the competing full arrangement or isolate texture-only reference material; the target's unmodified surroundings may already supply the needed material. Recheck actual spatial relationships, not just texture quality. |
| Data centers look approximately correct but detection fails | Check dark cores, light gaps, grayscale edges, highlights and shadows. Approximate centers and average grayscale do not prove readability. |
| Powder or hatching looks more natural but fails | Check intrusion into light bands, hollowed dark cores and density changes. Keep visual lessons without calling it finished. |
| High error correction still fails detection, or only the original passes | Revisit functional contours, gaps and display size rather than only raising version or correction level. |
| Rings decode but fragments fail | Keep the technical fallback without declaring the ring version a natural success. Change the carrier mechanism and retest; do not simply delete functional information. |
| A complete locator replacement helps, but its visible core and gaps were already acceptable | Isolate the enclosing contour, core, light gap and exterior separation in diagnostic controls before repainting all of them. A whole-region repair does not establish which part was necessary; retain existing material where the evidence supports it. |
| Only some sizes or compression variants pass | Retain the predefined tests. If evidence implicates functional contours, isolate regions or contour segments privately before combining repairs: a change can help one scale and hurt another. Recheck appearance, then fully retest the actual new file and every variant. |
| Decoding repairs expose three corners or a grid | Repeat appearance review and revisit layout, formation or material choice; technical success is insufficient. |
| Image changed, copying damaged it, or hash changed | Stop delivery and final saving. Export again, fully decode and bind a new report. |

Image models may enlarge the information area, crop the exterior or merge particles into large symbols. “Preserve exactly” in a prompt is not verification. A corrected SVG guide is technical material, not a finished artwork. Crops, supplied corners and pure-code detection cannot qualify delivery.

Retest changes to angle, information scale, exterior distribution, material and brightness. Mathematically preserved proportions do not guarantee stable detection. One successful color, size or calibration coefficient is not a universal recipe.

## Turn feedback into the next decision

Keep the detailed record private; transfer only reusable judgment into the Skill during the same round:

1. Bind the feedback or self-review to the exact version and distinguish a user judgment from an agent rejection. State the visible defect and location, the decoding evidence, and the cause still to be tested. A liked image can still have unresolved limitations.
2. Locate the failed decision. Inspect the returned image's actual geometry before placing information; prompt coordinates and requested object counts are not observations. When later tonal patches create eye rings, artificial mottling or a dedicated code surface, revisit carrier compatibility and functional geometry instead of treating the artefact as a texture problem.
3. Patch the smallest relevant guidance and apply it before retrying. If the rule already exists, move its check ahead of the operation that keeps violating it and specify observable evidence. For example, assess contact and shadows before spending time repairing readability, then assess appearance again after that repair.
4. Give each retry a diagnosed defect, a targeted change and a success check. If successive attempts reproduce the same structural conflict, change the information-forming mechanism instead of extending parameter sweeps or paid redraw loops. Ending that mechanism does not end an authorized creation task: pursue a different mechanism. Light, reflection or negative space are possible carriers, not a new default template. Recheck the original failure and both acceptance gates; record unresolved results and consult the revision in the next round.

Maintain language editions together. Do not pad the Skill with an entry for every picture or force a wording change when no new lesson exists. Record that the existing guidance was checked and applied instead. Methods that have not passed actual complete-artwork acceptance remain exploratory, not proven.
